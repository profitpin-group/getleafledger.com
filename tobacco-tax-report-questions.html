Monthly tobacco tax reports: the questions manufacturers and importers actually ask.

If you make or import cigars, pipe tobacco, hookah tobacco or roll-your-own, every month brings the same three reports and the same worry: will the inventory reconcile, will the excise tax tie out, and will FDA's numbers match TTB's? Which tobacco tax reports are due, and when

What monthly reports does a tobacco manufacturer or importer have to file?

A domestic factory files TTB F 5210.5, the monthly report of manufacturer operations (27 CFR 40.202), plus the excise tax return TTB F 5000.24. An importer files TTB F 5220.6, the monthly importer report (27 CFR 41.206), and pays import excise tax through CBP on the entry summary (CBP 7501). Both also file FDA Form 3852, the monthly user-fee report (21 CFR Part 1150), using the same removal and tax figures. The TTB 5210.5 guide and the 5220.6 importer guide walk through each one.

When is TTB Form 5210.5 due?

TTB lists the 5210.5 as due by the 20th day of the month after the reporting month (27 CFR 40.202). A report is required for every month, or part of a month, in which you operate as a manufacturer. Confirm the date on TTB's operational report due-date page, which is the current word on deadlines.

When is the TTB 5220.6 importer report due?

TTB lists the 5220.6 as due by the 15th day of the month after the reporting period (27 CFR 41.206), which is earlier than the manufacturer report. Importers that also hold a manufacturer permit therefore have two TTB deadlines in the same month. If you work to one month-end calendar, put the 15th first.

When is FDA Form 3852 due, and is that the same as paying the tobacco user fee?

FDA requires the prior month's removal and excise-tax information on Form 3852 by the 20th of each month. Filing the report and paying the user fee are separate steps: FDA uses everyone's reported tax figures to compute each company's share, then bills the assessment quarterly. The FDA 3852 guide covers the rows, columns and supporting documents.

Do I still have to file if we had no production, imports or sales this month?

Yes. TTB expects a report for every month you hold the permit, and TTB's importer guidance says a month with no imports is filed marked "no activity"; FDA 3852 is also still due for a zero month. A report with explicit zeros is a fact, while a missing report is a gap. LeafLedger treats an explicit zero-activity declaration differently from missing data, so an empty month is never assumed.

What happens if a TTB or FDA tobacco report is late or wrong?

Late excise tax returns and late payments can bring penalties and interest under the Internal Revenue Code (for example 26 U.S.C. 6651), and TTB examines the records behind the reports, not only the forms. On the FDA side, an unpaid user-fee assessment can cause a company's tobacco products to be treated as misbranded under the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. If you find an error after filing, correct it openly through the process the form instructions describe rather than folding it silently into next month; ask TTB or a qualified advisor how to handle your specific case.

When the inventory and the report won't reconcile

Why doesn't my book inventory match the physical count?

The usual causes are a removal recorded in the wrong month, a unit mix-up (cases against pounds or sticks), a missing in-bond receipt or transfer document, a product booked under the wrong class, or a count error. Treat the difference as a finding to explain, not a number to adjust, and do not enter a "loss" just to make book equal count. How do I reconcile tobacco inventory for the monthly TTB report?

Per product class and in one unit: opening on hand plus manufactured, received in bond and returned, minus every removal category, should equal book closing, and book closing should equal the physical count. Then multiply tax-paid removals by the class rate and compare the result with the tax on your return for the same month. The reconciliation steps in the 5210.5 guide list the six checks in order.

Why doesn't this month's opening balance match last month's closing?

Almost always because something was corrected after last month's report was filed and the correction was never reported, or the spreadsheet was edited back in place. Opening on hand should be last month's filed closing, carried forward, not re-derived from the current records. If a prior month changed, the change should be visible as a correction so the filed report and your records still agree.

How should shortages, overages and destroyed tobacco be reported?

The 5210.5 has lines for losses and for overages disclosed by inventory, and destruction must follow the notice or supervision rules in 27 CFR Part 40. Each entry needs a record behind it: a count, a destruction notice, a loss claim. A shortage with no explanation is something to investigate before filing, and the physical inventories themselves are reported on TTB F 5210.9 when the rules require one.

What is the difference between "removed tax-paid" and "removed without payment of tax"?

Tax-paid removals are product leaving the factory or bond for the domestic market; they drive the excise tax and the FDA 3852 figures. Removals without payment of tax include exports and in-bond transfers to another permittee, and each needs its supporting proof (export evidence, or the receiving permittee's documents). If the quantity is right but the document is missing, the tax-free treatment is not supported.

How do I handle pounds, sticks and cases on the same report?

Report each class in its own unit: sticks for cigars and cigarettes, pounds for pipe tobacco, roll-your-own, snuff and chewing tobacco. When production or shipping records use cases, cartons or tins, write the conversion factor down once and apply it the same way every month. Excise tax, hookah and pipe tobacco, and the FDA tie-out

How is federal excise tax calculated on cigars, pipe tobacco and roll-your-own?

The rates are set in 26 U.S.C. 5701 and listed on TTB's federal excise tax page. Since 2009 they have been $2.8311 per pound for pipe tobacco, $24.78 per pound for roll-your-own, $50.33 per thousand small cigars, and 52.75% of the sale price for large cigars, capped at 40.26 cents per cigar. Large cigars are the hard one, because the tax follows each cigar's sale price rather than a flat rate. Confirm the current rates on TTB's site before relying on these figures.

Why doesn't the tax on my 5000.24 return match the tax-paid removals on my 5210.5?

Check period cut-off first: the return is generally filed semimonthly (27 CFR 40.162), so the two periods together should cover the same calendar month as the 5210.5. Then check the rate table, the class each product was reported under, and, for large cigars, the sale prices used. The free roll-forward check multiplies tax-paid removals by the rate you enter and shows whether the result ties to your return.

Is hookah or shisha tobacco reported as pipe tobacco?

Hookah tobacco is commonly classified and taxed as pipe tobacco, but the classification depends on the product's appearance, type, packaging and labeling under the definitions in 26 U.S.C. 5702 and 27 CFR 40.11. A product suitable for and likely to be bought for rolling cigarettes can be roll-your-own, which is taxed at a much higher rate. The manufacturer or importer makes the first determination; TTB's Regulatory and Rulings Division makes the final one, so ask TTB if a product is borderline.

Why don't my FDA 3852 numbers match my TTB reports?

Form 3852 is meant to be built from the same month's TTB 5210.5 or 5220.6 and the 5000.24 or CBP 7501s, so a mismatch usually means a different period, a missed class roll-up (small plus large cigars go on one row), a unit difference, or excise tax mixed with duties or fees. FDA has also issued cigar-specific notices about reporting premium and non-premium cigars, so check the current notice on fda.gov. LeafLedger prepares the 3852 draft from the same reconciled records as the TTB drafts, so the two agree by construction or the difference is flagged.

How do importers tie CBP 7501 entries to the 5220.6 and FDA 3852?

Each entry summary carries the volume and the excise tax for what was entered or released for consumption; only the excise tax belongs on 3852, not duties or other fees. Entries dated in the month should roll up by class to the 5220.6 and the importer columns on 3852. Customs, inventory and accounting records often describe the same goods at different dates or units, so record the reason for every difference.

Templates, spreadsheets, software and records

Is there a TTB 5210.5 template or spreadsheet I can use?

TTB publishes the form, not a workbook, so most small manufacturers build their own sheet: one tab per product class, rows from opening on hand to closing, and a tab of written-down conversion factors. The spreadsheet guide shows the layout and the four ways a sheet drifts: units, rounding, back-editing and no trail. A spreadsheet is genuinely enough for one permit, one or two classes and no prior-period corrections.

How long do I have to keep the records behind tobacco tax reports?

27 CFR Part 40 sets a minimum of three years from the date of the report for manufacturers, and TTB can require longer.
